How I work

Four lessons from planning a 3-month programme in 4 weeks with a 10-person team across 4 time zones.

Clear ownership prevents bottlenecks

Every task has a named owner and a backup, so work never waits for someone to decide it is theirs.

Global coordination needs structure

Written handovers and a 3-tier escalation path keep work moving while teammates sleep.

Risk planning protects timelines

Risks go in a register with owners and backup plans before they turn into delays.

Documentation builds credibility

Plans, dashboards and reports that anyone can read at a glance, without asking me.

Planning a 3-month Masterclass

Project Management Associate internship, Team 2. I presented the final package.

The context

Design a structured virtual Masterclass programme on Time and Resource Management for students and young professionals, with scope, schedule, resources and monitoring all planned in 4 weeks.

Target client analysis for Grant Thornton China

Business Development internship, Team 3, which I led. Star Performer award.

The context

A 4-week remote mandate to find and qualify high-value potential clients for Grant Thornton China’s Shanghai and Beijing offices.

My role

I led the group, produced most of the deliverables myself and presented the final analysis.

What was built

A 35-firm shortlist (20 law firms, 5 private equity firms, 10 multinationals), a power and interest grid, use case maps, mind maps and a pain-point analysis for three priority firms.

What it shows

Research turned into a ranked pipeline with a reason, a contact route and a service match for every target.

Both projects were completed within the Excelerate global internship programme. The Project Management project was a team effort, and the Business Development project was led by me. The firms named are real public organisations, analysed from public information; no engagement with them took place.
